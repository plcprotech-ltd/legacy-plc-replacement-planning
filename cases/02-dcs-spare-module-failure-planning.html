Critical spares planning

Prioritize DCS spares by failure consequence, not by price alone.

This case uses a simple weighted matrix to identify modules that deserve on-site stock before a failure turns into a production outage.

Criticality model

Priority score: 4 — LOW

What to record for each module

FieldWhy it mattersExample decision
Exact catalog numberPrevents ordering a visually similar but incompatible revision.Photograph front label and rear I/O assembly.
Installed quantityShows fleet exposure.One spare may be insufficient across many cabinets.
Redundancy roleSeparates protected channels from single points.Prioritize non-redundant interfaces.
Firmware / revisionCompatibility can depend on version.Do not assume a later revision is drop-in.
Known-good spareInventory is only useful when condition is known.Record test status and storage location.
Lead-time evidenceTurns purchasing uncertainty into a measurable risk.Review periodically as platforms age.

Suggested score interpretation

4–6 · Low

Normal procurement planning may be enough. Keep configuration and part data current.

7–9 · Moderate

Review installed quantity, common failure modes and whether one tested spare should be held.

10–12 · High

Treat as a critical spare candidate and document a recovery procedure before failure occurs.
