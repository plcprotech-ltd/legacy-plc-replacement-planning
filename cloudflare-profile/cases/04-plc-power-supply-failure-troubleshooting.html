Fault isolation

Verify the power path before replacing the PLC power supply.

A dark rack or repeated power fault does not always mean the supply itself has failed. Use a documented sequence to separate incoming-power, protection, backplane, loading and module faults.

Safety: follow site lockout/tagout, electrical isolation and arc-flash procedures. Do not bypass protective devices or work energized unless site procedures explicitly permit it.

Fault-isolation sequence

1. Confirm the symptom

Record PSU indicators, controller state, affected racks, alarms and whether the fault is intermittent or permanent.

2. Verify incoming power

Check the correct supply source, disconnect state, protective device status and documented input-voltage range.

3. Inspect connectors

Look for loose terminals, damaged plugs, heat discoloration, contamination or mechanical strain.

4. Review rack loading

Compare the installed module load with the correct manufacturer power-budget method for that platform.

5. Isolate downstream faults

If procedures permit, use the manufacturer troubleshooting method to determine whether a module or backplane fault is collapsing the supply.

6. Preserve evidence

Record measurements, indicators, event logs and the exact replacement catalog number before changing hardware.
